# State coverage

What Grant Matcher covers in each of the 50 states and the District of Columbia, checked November 25, 2026. The 18 states whose pages are web pages were looked at again on November 26, 2026 (sections (g) and (h)), and Ren decided the held ones on November 27, 2026: Kentucky and Missouri were built (section (i)). The check of Connecticut, Georgia, Kansas and Michigan was finished on November 27 (section (j)), and Massachusetts and Mississippi, the last two unchecked, were checked the same day (section (k)). The state-by-state research behind section (b) was done the same day with anonymous requests only (no accounts, keys, sign-ups or terms accepted). Part of it ran under the user agent `grant-matcher-research` before the project settled on `grant-matcher (github.com/RendellRich/grant-matcher)`. One batch of that research (Louisiana to North Dakota) retried with a browser-style user agent after a 403, which the rule below doesn't allow. Its notes mark Maine's funding page as read only that way, so nothing from that page is used, and no status or reason on this page rests on such a retry. The registry that the code reads is `grantmatch/states.py`; this page explains it.

**The rule.** A page is read only after the host's robots.txt, with the project's own user agent. If robots.txt keeps out this crawler, ClaudeBot or the path, if the terms bar automated access or commercial reuse (Grant Matcher has paid subscription plans), if the site turns scripts away, or if neither can be confirmed, the page isn't used. For those sources this page keeps only the host and the reason. This rule was not met by all of the research: some pages were fetched before robots.txt or terms were read, including pages on hosts now blocked, and some rows in section (b) come from research that records no such check. For adeca.alabama.gov, agriculture.vermont.gov, the Louisiana arts division, cde.state.co.us, kansascommerce.gov and commerce.idaho.gov the research notes record that the content or cached copy was removed, and nothing here rests on it. For other blocked hosts the notes record no removal; their rows say why each is kept out and report nothing from its grant pages. Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Missouri, Montana and North Dakota were rechecked on November 25 with the project's own user agent, robots.txt before the pages on each host; their rows report only what that recheck saw.

**Short answer.** Federal grants, private foundations, community foundations and the county list cover all 50 states and DC. State grant lists cover 18 states and DC (reading Illinois' list is paused, section (f)): Florida, California, Illinois, Iowa, Ohio, Washington, West Virginia, New Jersey, New York, the District of Columbia and Minnesota (two agencies only, not a statewide list) and, from web pages read on November 26, Arkansas, Montana, North Dakota, Tennessee and Wyoming (each its arts council only) and Maryland (a short list on one department's page), and from November 27, Kentucky (its arts council only) and Missouri (three agencies' pages). In the other 32 states the site says plainly that there is no state catalog yet. Of those 32: 3 have a machine-readable list that Ren decided on 2026-11-25 to leave out (Alaska, Hawaii, Pennsylvania), for 11 the grant pages checked are web pages or a dashboard, not a data file: 1 has three dated calls entered by hand and no list built (Mississippi, section (k)), 7 gave no list this project could build from (Idaho, South Carolina and Utah, and Connecticut, Georgia, Kansas and Michigan, whose check was finished on November 27, section (j)), 2 were skipped by Ren's decision (Oklahoma, South Dakota) and 1 waits on a permission request (Virginia; section (g)), 15 are **blocked** (robots.txt, terms or a refusal to scripts keep the key pages out; for Massachusetts, two calls from a host it may read are entered by hand), 3 had no current list anywhere the research looked, and none is unchecked. State calls are also entered by hand for Connecticut, Kansas, Massachusetts and Mississippi. County and city programs are hand-kept for some counties and cities in 9 states ([county-coverage.md](county-coverage.md)).

## (b) Each state

Status (the registry's value in brackets):

- **built** (`built`): an adapter reads the state's own list. **built this PR** marks New York, the District of Columbia and Minnesota. **built (web pages)** marks the states built on November 26 and 27, 2026 from a few agencies' pages or a short list; none of them is a statewide list.
- **left out by decision** (`decide`): a machine-readable list works but isn't built, because Ren decided on 2026-11-25 to leave it out; section (c) gives the reasons.
- **HTML only** (`html`): the grant pages checked are web pages, a dashboard or a bid system, not a data file.
- **blocked** (`blocked`): robots.txt or the terms keep this crawler or this use out, or the site turns scripts away, for the pages that would carry the state's calls. Nothing is read from them, and only the host and the reason are recorded here.
- **none found** (`none`): nowhere the research looked has a current public list of state grant calls: past awards only, a login wall, or a list that is empty or abandoned.
- **unchecked** (`unchecked`): the research couldn't establish the answer. Nothing is claimed; the page's note says only that the state's grants aren't read in yet.
- `next` (an adapter worth building next) is in the registry but no state has it now.

## (d) Refresh job

The weekly job approved on 2026-11-25 is `.github/workflows/refresh-states.yml` ("Refresh state lists"). It runs weekly, Mondays at 13:17 UTC (cron `17 13 * * 1`), and it can also be run by hand. Since November 25 on main it runs `grantmatch.refresh_states`, which takes its list from the registry (commit 672e8a5; the same commit made `skipped-report.yml` download the skipped lists of West Virginia, New Jersey, New York, DC and Minnesota). The rest of this section's first paragraphs describe the job as first added. One job on ubuntu-latest reads both lists (`python -m grantmatch.westvirginia --out build/westvirginia.json.gz --skipped build/skipped-westvirginia.json.gz`, and the same for `grantmatch.newjersey`), embeds them (`python -m grantmatch.embed programs`), and uploads six files to the data-latest release: `westvirginia.json.gz`, `westvirginia.npz`, `skipped-westvirginia.json.gz`, `newjersey.json.gz`, `newjersey.npz` and `skipped-newjersey.json.gz`. `tests/test_states.py` reads the file and checks the cron, the commands and the uploaded names against `grantmatch/states.py`.

Until its first run, West Virginia and New Jersey ship from the committed snapshots `data/westvirginia.json.gz` and `data/newjersey.json.gz`, made on November 25, 2026. `export_web.fetch()` asks the release for `westvirginia.json.gz`, gets a 404, and reads the file in `data/` instead (tested in `tests/test_states.py`). Until the job has published vectors, `program_vectors()` embeds the records during the export, as it already does for the hand-kept lists. A snapshot ages. `export_web` leaves out any call whose closing date is before the day of the export (`still_open()`, for every open-call state; Florida's catalog and the hand-kept lists keep their own rules), so expired calls stop showing, and the state tab says when its data was last updated. Calls the state adds after the snapshot don't appear until the job runs (or the snapshot is remade with `python -m grantmatch.westvirginia --out data/westvirginia.json.gz` and `python -m grantmatch.newjersey --out data/newjersey.json.gz`). On November 25 the New Jersey snapshot had 9 dated calls (the first closing December 4) and 22 rolling ones; West Virginia's 7 close between December 15, 2026 and June 30, 2027, except one ongoing.

**How new data reaches the site.** "Refresh state lists" is not in `deploy-site.yml`'s `workflow_run` list (that list names the federal, Florida, California, Illinois, Iowa, Ohio, Washington, foundations and funder website refreshes), and `deploy-site.yml` is unchanged, so the weekly job doesn't start a deploy itself. The next deploy picks the new files up, because the export reads the release at every deploy, and the federal refresh triggers one twice a day. So new West Virginia and New Jersey data reaches the site within about 12 hours of the weekly run. Until the job's first run, a remade snapshot also reaches the site on the next deploy (the deploy workflow's push paths don't include the two files, so pushing one starts no deploy); after that run the release copy is used instead.

Weekly means a call a state opens mid-week can show up to a week late. Closed calls still drop out at every site export, which runs several times a day, because `still_open()` checks each record's closing date against the export day, not the refresh day.

Later states join the same job as more lines in the two steps, so the job count doesn't grow with each state. A proposed version that runs New York, DC and Minnesota too is in [proposed-workflows/refresh-states.yml.txt](proposed-workflows/refresh-states.yml.txt) and isn't in use ([freshness.md](freshness.md)). Two workflow changes were not made then. First, `deploy-site.yml`: its push paths list `grantmatch/export_web.py` but not `grantmatch/states.py`, which the export now reads, so a change to the registry alone wouldn't rebuild the site until the next refresh-triggered deploy. Second, `skipped-report.yml` (since made, see above): until it changed, West Virginia and New Jersey stayed out of the Friday skipped grants report, because it neither downloads their skipped lists nor passes their data files. The change, not made: add `-p 'skipped-westvirginia.json.gz' -p 'skipped-newjersey.json.gz'` to the first `gh release download`, `-p westvirginia.json.gz -p newjersey.json.gz` to the second, and `--westvirginia build/westvirginia.json.gz --newjersey build/newjersey.json.gz` to the `python -m grantmatch.skipped` line. `grantmatch/skipped.py` already takes those arguments and reads the two skipped lists when they are there (both come from `grantmatch/states.py`). Their registry entries have `"in_skip_report": False`, so the report doesn't call their files missing meanwhile; once `skipped-report.yml` downloads them, setting it to true makes the report say when a refresh failed to publish one. The report job's minutes don't change measurably (a few more seconds of download).
